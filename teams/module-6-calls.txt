Calls in Teams

Module 6: Working Smarter in Teams

Make audio and video calls to coworkers from a chat or the Calls app, use the call controls, know when a call beats a message, and understand what calling phone numbers requires.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Start an audio or video call from a chat, or call someone from the Calls app
- Use the call controls: Mic, Camera, Share, and Leave, with their shortcuts
- Decide when a quick call works better than a chat, and follow up in writing
- Explain that calls between Teams users at work are built in, while calling phone numbers needs Teams Phone


## When a call beats a chat
 type: scenario
 role: You are the front office assistant at Lakeside Medical Associates.
 text: The check-in label printer is jammed again. You and Luis in IT have traded nine chat messages (“Is the light blinking?” “Which light?”) while patients wait in line. A two-minute call would have fixed it.
 task: Know when to call instead of typing, place the call quickly, and confirm what you agreed in writing afterward.

Teams calls are one-on-one or small-group audio or video conversations that you start right away, without scheduling a meeting. They work best for something urgent, something easier to show than to describe, or a back-and-forth that is going nowhere in chat. If the person's status is Busy or Do not disturb and it is not urgent, send a message instead.

1. From a chat: open the chat and select Video call or Audio call at the top right. To call several people, start a group chat with them first (calls from a chat work for up to 20 people).
2. From the Calls app: select Calls on the app bar, type a name in the box, and select the person to call. You can also call from someone's profile card.
3. To answer an incoming call, choose video or audio on the call notification (Ctrl+Shift+A accepts with video, Ctrl+Shift+S with audio).


## Call controls
 type: table
 caption: Call controls work like meeting controls
 columns:
   Control
   Shortcut (Windows)
   Use it to
 rows:
    Mic
    Ctrl+Shift+M
    Mute while the front desk is noisy
    Camera
    Ctrl+Shift+O
    Turn on video to show something
    Share
    Ctrl+Shift+E
    Show one window, such as an error message
    Leave
    Ctrl+Shift+H
    Hang up

Note: Calls between Teams users in your organization are built into Teams. Calling regular phone numbers from Teams is different: your organization needs Teams Phone licenses and a phone service connected to Teams. If you see a dial pad in Calls, your organization has set that up for you.

 type: worked-example
 title: Fixing the printer with one call
 task: Get the label printer working before the next patient reaches the window.
 steps:
   move: In Calls, type Luis and select Luis Ortega to call him.
   why: Showing and talking is faster than describing lights in a chat.
   move: Mute while Luis checks the printer log on his end.
   why: Your microphone would pick up the waiting room. Mute until you need to talk.
   move: When it works, unmute, thank him, and hang up with Leave.
   why: Short calls end when the problem is solved.
   move: Send a chat: “Printer fixed: Luis cleared the jam and replaced the roller.”
   why: A written note tells anyone who checks later what happened. Calls leave no record unless you write one.
 result: The printer works, and the fix is written down.
 takeaway: Call to solve, then write to confirm.

Practice: Sort each situation.


## Knowledge check
Check yourself: Where do you start an audio call with someone you are already chatting with? Answer: Audio call at the top right of the chat. Every chat has Video call and Audio call at the top right.

Check yourself: Which is true about calling phone numbers from Teams at work? Answer: It needs Teams Phone licenses and a phone service your organization sets up. Teams-to-Teams calls are built in. Calling regular phone numbers needs Teams Phone and a connected phone service.
