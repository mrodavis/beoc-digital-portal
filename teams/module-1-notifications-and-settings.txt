Notifications, Activity, and Settings

Module 1: Getting Started with Teams

Control which Teams notifications reach you and how, mute busy chats, set channel notifications, and set up and test your headset before your first call.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Use the Activity feed to catch up on what needs you
- Choose where each kind of notification appears in Settings › Notifications and activity
- Mute a chat and set channel notifications for the channels you follow
- Choose a speaker and microphone in Settings › Devices and make a test call


## Too many pings
 type: scenario
 role: You are the front office assistant at Lakeside Medical Associates.
 text: By 10:00 AM you have had 26 pop-up banners: lunch orders, potluck sign-ups, and reactions to a message you sent yesterday. In the middle of them was Maria's question about Friday coverage, and you almost missed it. Your new headset also arrived, and your first call with IT is this afternoon.
 task: Set up notifications so the messages that need you stand out, and get your headset working before the call.

Teams can tell you about something in two places: a banner (a pop-up on your screen) and the Activity feed. The Activity feed keeps a list of @mentions, replies to your posts, reactions, and missed calls. Banners interrupt you; the feed waits for you. A good notification plan sends the important things to both and the nice-to-know things to the feed only, or nowhere.

- Open Activity on the app bar to catch up. Unread items are bold; select one to jump to the exact message.
- Use the filters at the top of the feed (such as Unread and @Mentions) to see only what needs you.
- An @mention of your name is the clearest sign that someone needs an answer from you.


## Choose where each notification appears
Select Settings and more (…) next to your picture › Settings › Notifications and activity. For each kind of notification you can choose where it appears or turn it off. You also set meeting notifications here, such as alerts when a meeting starts and notifications for meeting chats.

 caption: A notification plan for the front desk (the kinds of notifications in Settings › Notifications and activity › Chats and channels and Meetings)
 columns:
   Kind of notification
   Suggested setting
   Why

    Mentions of me
    Activity feed and banner
    Someone needs an answer from you
    Chat messages
    Activity feed and banner
    Most chats at work are questions for you
    Replies to my posts
    Activity feed and banner
    Follow-ups on what you started
    Reactions to my messages
    Off (or Activity feed only)
    Nice to see, rarely needs action
    Meeting chat
    Activity feed only
    Meeting chats can be busy; read them when you join

Two quicker controls handle individual conversations. To quiet one chat, point to it, select More options (…) › Mute. You stop getting notifications but can still read it. For a channel, point to it and select More options (…) › Channel notifications. You can turn notifications for new posts on or off and choose whether they appear as a banner and in the feed or only in the feed.

Practice: Sort each notification into the setting that fits a front office assistant.


## Set up your audio before the first call
Plug in your headset, then open Settings and more (…) › Settings › Devices. Choose the headset in the Speaker list and in the Microphone list, check Noise suppression, and select Make a test call. Teams asks you to record a short message and plays it back, then shows the results for your microphone, speaker, camera, and network. Fixing a wrong microphone here takes ten seconds; fixing it in front of six people on a call takes much longer.

 type: worked-example
 title: Getting ready for the 2:00 PM call with IT
 task: Make sure the new headset works before the call starts.
 steps:
   move: Open Settings and more (…) › Settings › Devices.
   why: Devices is where Teams decides which speaker, microphone, and camera to use for every call and meeting.
   move: Choose Headset Earphone (Jabra Evolve2 40) as the speaker and Headset Microphone (Jabra Evolve2 40) as the microphone.
   why: Teams may keep using the laptop's built-in speaker and microphone until you tell it otherwise. The built-in microphone also picks up the whole waiting room.
   move: Select Make a test call and listen to the playback.
   why: Hearing yourself confirms that both directions work before anyone else is waiting on you.
 result: When the IT call starts, your audio works the first time.
 takeaway: Set up and test new audio equipment before the call, not during it.

Note: While you are in Settings, look at Appearance and accessibility: you can choose Follow operating system theme, Light, or Dark. You will see more accessibility settings in Module 6.


## Knowledge check
Check yourself: You want to keep reading the Front Desk Lunch Orders group chat but stop it from interrupting you. What should you do? Answer: Mute the chat. Muting stops notifications for that chat but keeps it in your list so you can read it whenever you want.

Check yourself: Where do you choose the speaker and microphone Teams uses and make a test call? Answer: Settings › Devices. Settings › Devices controls your speaker, microphone, camera, and noise suppression, and has Make a test call.
