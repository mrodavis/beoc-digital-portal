Write, Reply, Forward, and Attach

Module 6: Gmail

Compose clear messages, choose To, Cc, or Bcc, pick Reply, Reply all, or Forward, send files as attachments or Google Drive links, and undo a message you just sent.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Compose a message and decide who goes in To, Cc, and Bcc
- Choose Reply, Reply all, or Forward for the situation
- Attach a file from your computer, and insert a Google Drive file as a link with the right access
- Explain the 25 MB attachment limit for personal Gmail and what Gmail does with larger files
- Undo a message right after you send it


## Writing a new message
 type: scenario
 role: You are the office assistant at Parkview Property Management.
 text: Andre's showing moved to Thursday and the whole team needs to know. A plumbing invoice has to get to Priya, the bookkeeper. A tenant needs a repair notice, three tenants need the same water shutoff notice, and Denise wants a walkthrough video that is far too big to attach.
 task: Send each message to exactly the right people, with the right files, the first time.

1. At the top left, select Compose. A New Message window opens at the bottom right.
2. In To, type a name or address. Pick the person from the suggestions. To add more than one, put a comma between addresses.
3. To copy someone, select Cc. To add someone privately, select Bcc.
4. Add a short, specific subject, such as “Plumbing repair scheduled for Unit 4B”.
5. Write the message, then select Send at the bottom.

Note: Tenant, customer, and client email addresses are private information. When you send one message to many people who don't know each other, put them in Bcc and put your own address (or the office's) in To.


## Reply, Reply all, or Forward
Open a message, and below it you'll see Reply, Reply all (when there's more than one recipient), and Forward. Reply sends your answer only to the sender. Reply all sends it to the sender and everyone in the To and Cc lines. Forward sends the message, with its attachments, to someone new, and you can add a note at the top.

Practice: Drag the right choice onto each situation.

Before you select Reply all, look at who is on the message. A reply meant for one person that goes to the whole office is one of the most common workplace email mistakes. You can change which button is the default in Settings › See all settings › General › Default reply behavior.


## Attachments and Google Drive links
To attach a file from your computer, select Attach files (the paper clip) at the bottom of the compose window, choose the file, and select Open. In a personal Gmail account, attachments can total up to 25 MB; in a work or school account, your Google Workspace administrator sets the limit. If a file is larger than the limit, Gmail doesn't attach it. It adds a Google Drive link instead.

To share a file that's already in Google Drive, select Insert files using Drive (the Drive icon), pick the file, and select Insert. A Drive link works for any file in Drive, including Google Docs, Sheets, Slides, and Forms. Sending a Drive file as an attachment works only for files that weren't created in Docs, Sheets, Slides, or Forms. When you send, Gmail checks whether your recipients can open the file. If they can't, it prompts you to change the file's sharing settings first. If you choose Anyone with the link, you pick whether people can view, comment in, or edit the file; to limit access to the people on the email, select More options.

 type: worked-example
 title: Sending the Tenant Handbook to a new applicant
 task: Rosa Delgado applied for an apartment and needs the Tenant Handbook, a Google Doc in the office Drive.
 steps:
   move: Compose a message to Rosa and select Insert files using Drive.
   why: The handbook is a Google Doc, so it can only be sent as a Drive link.
   move: Pick Tenant Handbook and select Insert, then Send.
   why: Gmail checks access when you send.
   move: Gmail prompts you to change the file's sharing settings. Select More options, limit access to the email's recipients, and give view access. Then send.
   why: Rosa needs to read the handbook, not change it. View access for just the people on the email keeps the office's copy safe; Anyone with the link would let anyone who gets the link open it.
 result: Rosa can open and read the current handbook, and only the office can edit it.
 takeaway: Share Drive files with the least access the person needs: view to read, comment to suggest, edit only for coworkers who maintain the file.


## Undo Send
Right after you select Send, a “Message sent” notice appears at the bottom left with Undo and View message. Select Undo and Gmail cancels the send and reopens your message so you can fix it. You only have a few seconds. To give yourself more time, go to Settings › See all settings and, next to Undo Send, choose a cancellation period of 5, 10, 20, or 30 seconds, then select Save Changes at the bottom.

Note: Undo Send works because Gmail waits those few seconds before it actually sends. Once the period is over, the message is gone and can't be pulled back, so read it once more before you select Send.


## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: You need to email 12 tenants about a parking lot repaving. They don't know each other. How should you address it? Answer: Put your own address in To and the 12 tenants in Bcc. Bcc keeps each tenant's address private, and Reply all from a tenant won't reach the others.

Check yourself: In your personal Gmail, you attach a 180 MB video. What happens? Answer: Gmail adds a Google Drive link instead of an attachment. Files over the 25 MB limit are sent as Google Drive links.

Check yourself: Andre emailed you and Denise, with Kim in Cc. Only Andre needs your answer. Which button? Answer: Reply. Reply goes only to the sender. Reply all would send your answer to Denise and Kim too.
