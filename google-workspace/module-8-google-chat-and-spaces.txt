Message Your Team in Google Chat

Module 8: Working Together

Use Google Chat for quick team conversations: direct messages, spaces, @mentions, reactions, and sharing Drive files with the right access.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Open Google Chat in Gmail and start a direct message or a group message
- Create a space and add people to it
- Mention someone with @, and decide whether to add them to the space
- Share a Google Drive file in a message and choose the access people get
- Decide when a message belongs in Chat and when it belongs in email


## Quick questions, without the email thread
 type: scenario
 role: You're coordinating the Riverside Commons lobby repaint.
 text: The repaint starts next week. Kim needs to check the lobby lights, Andre needs to know prospects should use the side door, and everyone needs the repaint schedule. Ten short emails would bury the office. Parkview uses Google Chat for quick team conversations like these.
 task: Send a direct message, post updates in the team space, share the schedule, and set up a new space.

Google Chat is Google's messaging app. You can use it at chat.google.com or inside Gmail: select Chat on the far left of Gmail. Chat has direct messages (DMs) between two or more people, and spaces, where a team or project keeps its conversation, files, and members in one place.

 type: key-terms
 terms:
   term: Direct message (DM)
   definition: A private conversation with one person, or a group DM with a few people.
   term: Space
   definition: A named place for a team or project conversation. People can be added or leave, and new members see the conversation.
   term: @mention
   definition: Typing @ and a name in a message so that person gets a notification.
   term: Reaction
   definition: An emoji you add to a message to answer without writing a new one.
   term: Shortcuts
   definition: Home, Mentions, and Starred at the top of Chat's left side. Mentions lists messages where someone mentioned you.

Note: Microsoft Teams users: Google Chat's direct messages are like Teams chats, and spaces are like channels in a team.


## Direct messages
1. In Gmail, select Chat on the far left (or open chat.google.com).
2. Select New chat at the top left.
3. Enter a name or email address. For a group message, add more names.
4. Select Start chat, type your message in the box at the bottom, and select Send (or press Enter).

The first time you message someone, they may need to accept your message request before you can chat. If your work account allows chatting with people outside your organization, their names show an External badge, so you always know when a conversation includes outsiders.


## Spaces for teams and projects
1. Select New chat › Create a space.
2. Enter a space name. You can also choose an emoji for the space's picture.
3. Select Create.
4. In the space, select Add people, pick the people, and select Add.

A space keeps a project together: the conversation, the files people share, and the list of members. When the lobby repaint is over, the space still holds the whole history. With a personal Google Account you can create spaces for collaborating with others; work accounts may have more kinds of spaces, depending on your organization.

Practice: Sort each conversation into the best place.


## Mentions, reactions, and files
 type: table
 caption: Everyday Chat actions
 columns:
   To
   Do this
 rows:
    Get one person's attention
    Type @ and their name, and pick them from the list. They get a notification.
    Mention someone who isn't in the space
    Chat asks: Add member and send (adds them and notifies them) or Send message only.
    Notify everyone in the space
    Type @all. Use it only when everyone really needs to see the message.
    Answer with an emoji
    Point to the message and add a reaction.
    Share a Drive file
    Type @ and part of the file name, and pick the file under Files. If someone can't open it, Chat asks you to select the type of access.
    Share a file from your computer
    Upload file, or drag the file into the message box (up to 200 MB).

 type: worked-example
 title: Sharing the repaint schedule
 task: Get the schedule to everyone in the Riverside Commons Team space.
 steps:
   move: Type “Here's the repaint schedule.” then @Lobby, and pick Lobby Repaint Schedule under Files.
   why: The message carries a link to the one current file, not a copy.
   move: Press Enter. Chat says some members can't open the file and asks for the type of access.
   why: Sharing in Chat also shares the file in Drive.
   move: Choose Viewer and select Send.
   why: The team needs to read the schedule, not change it. Give the least access that does the job.
 result: Everyone in the space can open the schedule, and it stays up to date when Denise changes it.
 takeaway: Share the Drive file, not a downloaded copy, and pick the access level on purpose.

Note: Chat is still work communication. Don't post tenants' private information, such as bank details or Social Security numbers, in a space, and remember that people added later can read the space's history.

## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: You @mention Andre in a space he isn't in yet, and you want him to see the conversation. Which option do you choose? Answer: Add member and send. Add member and send adds Andre to the space and notifies him.

Check yourself: Where should a project's ongoing team conversation go? Answer: A space with the project's team. A space keeps the conversation, members, and shared files together, and new members can catch up.

Check yourself: You share a Drive file in Chat, and Chat says some members can't open it. What does that mean? Answer: You need to choose the type of access to give them, or they won't be able to open it. Chat checks the file's sharing and asks you to select the type of access for people who don't have it.
