Charts and Pivot Tables

Module 4: Google Sheets

Turn numbers into a chart with Insert › Chart and the chart editor, and summarize a long list with a pivot table.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Insert a chart from selected data and change its type in the chart editor's Setup tab
- Add a chart title in the Customize tab
- Create a pivot table on a new sheet and add rows, columns, and values
- Explain when a chart or a pivot table is the better tool


## Charts
 type: scenario
 role: You prepare numbers for the monthly owners' meeting.
 text: Marcus presents to the building owners each month. He wants a chart of repair requests by month, and a summary of rent by building from the 40-row rent roll, without retyping any numbers.
 task: Make the chart and the summary from the data you already have.

1. Select the data, including the labels (for example, Month and Repairs).
2. Insert › Chart. Sheets picks a chart type and opens the chart editor on the right.
3. On the Setup tab, choose a different Chart type if you need one, and check the data range.
4. On the Customize tab, open Chart & axis title to type a title and change its font.
5. Drag the chart to move it; drag the blue markers to resize it. Double-click it to reopen the chart editor.

## Pivot tables
A pivot table summarizes a long list: total rent by building, number of repairs by type, average cost by vendor. You do not write formulas; you choose which column to group by and which to add up.

1. Select the data. Every column needs a header.
2. Insert › Pivot table. Check the data range, choose New sheet, and click Create.
3. In the pivot table editor on the right, next to Rows, click Add and pick a column to group by (Building).
4. Next to Values, click Add and pick the column to summarize (Rent). Sheets adds it up with SUM; choose another way, like AVERAGE or COUNTA, under Summarize by.
5. Add a field under Columns to split the summary across the top (Status).
6. The pivot table updates when the source data changes. Click it and choose Edit to reopen the editor.

Note: Sheets may show Suggested pivot tables in the editor. They can be a good start; check that they answer your question.


## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: Marcus needs total rent for each building from a 40-row rent roll. What is fastest? Answer: A pivot table with Building in Rows and Rent in Values. A pivot table groups the rows by building and adds the rents for you.

Check yourself: Where do you change a chart's title? Answer: Customize tab › Chart & axis title. Titles and fonts are on the Customize tab of the chart editor.
