Transitions, Animations, and Speaker Notes

Module 5: Google Slides

Add slide transitions and object animations in the Motion panel, control when animations start, and write speaker notes for whoever presents.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Add a transition to one slide or to every slide
- Animate an object, choose when the animation starts, and play it back
- Show the speaker notes area and write notes for the presenter
- Use motion so it helps the audience instead of distracting them

## Add a transition
1. Select the slide in the filmstrip.
2. Select Slide › Transition. The Motion panel opens on the right.
3. Under Slide transition, choose a transition, such as Dissolve. Drag the speed slider to make it faster or slower.
4. To use the same transition everywhere, select Apply to all slides.
5. Select Play to preview it.


## Animate an object
1. Select the object on the slide, such as the bulleted text.
2. Select Insert › Animation. In the Motion panel, a new animation is added (Appear, On click, by default).
3. Choose the animation, such as Fade in.
4. Choose when it starts: On click, After previous, or With previous. For text, check By paragraph to bring in one bullet at a time.
5. Drag the speed slider if needed, and select Play to preview.

Practice: Drag the right start option onto each situation.

Use one or two kinds of motion for the whole presentation. If every slide spins, bounces, and flies, people watch the motion instead of reading the message.


## Speaker notes
Speaker notes sit under each slide. They're for the presenter: reminders, details, and what to hand out. The audience doesn't see them during the slideshow. If the notes area is hidden, choose View › Show speaker notes, then click in it (it says Click to add speaker notes) and type.


 type: worked-example
 title: Notes for the First Week slide
 task: Help whoever presents the welcome presentation cover everything on slide 2.
 steps: 
   move: Keep the slide short: three bullet points.
   why: Tenants read the slide; the presenter adds the details.
   move: In the notes write “Hand out the key fob form here.”
   why: It's an action for the presenter not something tenants need to read on screen.
   move: Add the office phone hours to the notes.
   why: If someone asks the presenter has the answer without adding clutter to the slide.
 result: A clean slide for the audience and a script for the presenter.
 takeaway: Put what the audience must see on the slide; put what the presenter must say or do in the notes.



## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: You want the bullet points on slide 2 to appear one at a time when you click. What do you add? Answer: An animation on the text, starting On click, by paragraph. An animation on the text box set to On click, with By paragraph checked, brings in one bullet per click.

Check yourself: What's the best use of speaker notes? Answer: Reminders and details for the presenter. Speaker notes are for the presenter and aren't shown on the audience's screen.
