Phishing, Junk Email, and Blocked Senders

Module 6: Working Smarter and Safely

Recognize phishing, report it the right way, keep junk out of your Inbox, rescue real mail from Junk Email, and manage blocked and safe senders.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Recognize common signs of a phishing message
- Report phishing and junk from new Outlook
- Block a sender and add a safe sender
- Move a real message out of Junk Email


## Why the front desk is a target
 type: scenario
 role: You check the Lakeside front desk inbox first thing Monday.
 text: A message that looks like it's from IT says your mailbox is full and asks you to verify your password. A gift card offer, three glove promotions, and a real imaging confirmation stuck in Junk Email are waiting too.
 task: Handle each message the safe way: report, block, or rescue.

Phishing messages pretend to come from someone you trust to get you to click a link, open an attachment, or type a password. Medical offices are common targets because they handle health and payment information, and front desk staff open messages from strangers all day.

 type: table
 caption: Signs of phishing
 columns:
   Sign
   Example
 rows:
    Urgency or threats
    "Verify your password within 24 hours or your account will be closed"
    A look-alike sender
    it-support@lakesidemed-help.example.net instead of the real lakesidemed.example.com
    Rewards or refunds
    "Click this link to get your tax refund!"
    Fake shared files and invoices
    A document that asks for your email and password to open, or an invoice for something you never ordered
    Outlook warnings
    A '?' in the sender's picture or a "via" tag when Outlook can't verify the sender

Point to a link without clicking to see where it really goes. If a message claims to be from someone you know, check with them by phone using a number you already have, not one in the message.


## Report, block, and rescue
- Report phishing: select the message, then Report › Report phishing. Reporting tells Microsoft about the sender but doesn't block it.
- Report junk: Report › Report junk for unwanted ads and spam.
- Block a sender: Settings › Mail › Junk email. Under Blocked senders, type the address and select Add (or add a whole domain under Blocked domains), then select Save.
- Safe senders: in the same place, add addresses or domains you always want in the Inbox, then select Save.
- Rescue real mail: open the Junk Email folder, select the message, and select Not junk. It moves back to the Inbox.

Note: Follow your office's policy too. Many organizations want suspected phishing reported to IT as well, especially if you already clicked a link or typed a password. Report it right away; speed limits the damage.

Note: Classic Outlook: Home tab › Junk › Block Sender, Never Block Sender, or Junk E-mail Options. A Report button may also be available, depending on your organization.

Practice: Sort each message by the right action.


## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: You report a message as phishing. Will that sender's next message be blocked? Answer: No. Reporting doesn't block; add the sender to Blocked senders to stop their mail. Reporting and blocking are separate. Block the sender in Settings › Mail › Junk email.

Check yourself: A message from "Lakeside IT Support" comes from it-support@lakesidemed-help.example.net and asks for your password. What's the safest move? Answer: Report it as phishing and don't click anything. The domain isn't Lakeside's, and IT never needs your password by email. Report it.
