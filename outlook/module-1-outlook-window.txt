Finding Your Way Around Outlook

Module 1: Getting Started with Outlook

Learn the parts of the new Outlook window, move between Mail, Calendar, People and To Do, and arrange the window so the front desk inbox is easy to work through.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Identify the title bar, Ribbon, navigation bar, folder pane, message list and reading pane in new Outlook
- Switch between Mail, Calendar, People and To Do
- Show or hide the folder pane and move the reading pane
- Use the Search box, the Settings button and My Day


## A window built for scanning

New Outlook arranges everything from left to right in the order you use it: pick an app, pick a folder, pick a message, read it. Knowing the names of the parts matters, because help articles and coworkers use them: "select the folder pane," "open the reading pane."

## The Ribbon in new Outlook
When you are reading mail, the Ribbon has three tabs: Home for working with messages (New mail, Delete, Archive, Report, Sweep, Move to, Reply, Flag and more), View for arranging the window and opening View settings, and Help. New Outlook shows commands in a single row. When the window is too narrow for all of them, the rest move under More options, the three dots (…) at the right end of the row.

- When you start writing a message, new tabs appear: Message, Insert, Format text and Options. They disappear again when you send or discard the message.
- In Calendar and People, the Home tab changes to show the commands for that app, such as New event or New contact.
- Hover over a command to see its name. Commands with a small arrow open a menu.

Note: Classic Outlook: the Ribbon has more tabs (File, Home, Send / Receive, Folder, View, Help) and groups commands in labeled boxes. The navigation bar may be on the left edge or under the folder pane, depending on the version.


## Arranging the window
- Show or hide the folder pane with the button with three lines at the left end of the Ribbon tab row. Hiding it gives the message list more room on a small screen.
- Move the reading pane with View tab › Layout: show it on the right, on the bottom, or hide it. On a wide screen, the right side lets you see more messages at once.
- My Day, at the top right next to Notifications, opens a pane with today's calendar and your To Do tasks, so you can see your schedule while you read mail.
- Settings, the gear at the top right, opens every setting in one place, grouped as Accounts, General, Mail, Calendar and People. View tab › View settings opens the same page.

Search sits at the top of the window no matter which app you are in. Type a name, a subject word or a claim number and press Enter. You will learn search filters in Module 3.


 type: worked-example
 title: Putting the front desk window back the way you work
 task: Omar left the reading pane at the bottom and the folder pane hidden. Restore your layout before the next patient arrives.
 steps: 
   move: Select the three-line button at the left end of the Ribbon tab row.
   why: That button shows or hides the folder pane. You need it to reach Junk Email and your own folders.
   move: On the View tab open Layout and choose to show the reading pane on the right.
   why: Side by side you can scan the list and read the selected message at the same time which is faster on a wide monitor.
   move: Open My Day from the top right.
   why: The front desk runs on the schedule. My Day keeps today's appointments in view while you work through the inbox.
 result: Folder pane showing reading pane on the right today's calendar in My Day. About four clicks.
 takeaway: Shared computers drift. Learn where the layout controls are so you can reset a window in seconds instead of working around it.



## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: Where do you switch from Mail to Calendar in new Outlook? Answer: The navigation bar at the far left. The navigation bar on the left edge switches between Mail, Calendar, People and To Do.

Check yourself: A command you need isn't showing on the Home tab because the window is narrow. Where do you look? Answer: More options (…) at the right end of the command row. New Outlook shows one row of commands. Commands that don't fit move under More options, the three dots at the right end.
