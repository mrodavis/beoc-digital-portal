Folders, Archive, and Favorites

Module 3: Organizing Your Inbox

Design a folder structure that matches how a medical front desk works, create folders and subfolders, move several messages at once, and keep your busiest folders in Favorites.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Plan a small folder structure based on how the office works
- Create folders and subfolders in new Outlook
- Move one or several messages into a folder
- Add a folder to Favorites and use Archive for finished mail


## The Inbox is a to-do list

 type: scenario
 role: You are organizing the Lakeside front desk mailbox.
 text: The Inbox has 140 messages: referral confirmations insurance notices from three plans vendor invoices newsletters and a few messages that actually need you today. Denise asks for a system so that anyone covering the desk can find a referral in seconds.
 task: Build a short list of folders that matches the work and keep the Inbox for messages that still need action.


A good folder structure follows the work, not the sender. Ask what question you will be answering when you look for a message later: "Did the imaging center confirm the referral?" points to a Referrals folder. Keep it short. Five to eight top-level folders are easier to use than thirty, and search (Lesson 4) finds the rest.

## Create folders and move messages
- Top-level folder: right-click your email address in the folder pane (or point to it and select More options) and choose Create new folder. Type the name and press Enter. You can also select Create new folder at the bottom of your folder list.
- Subfolder: right-click the folder it belongs in, such as Inbox or Insurance & Billing, and choose Create new subfolder.
- Move a message: select it, then Home tab › Move to, and pick the folder. You can also drag it onto the folder, or right-click it › Move.
- Move several at once: click the first message, then hold Ctrl and click the others. Shift+click selects everything between two messages.
- Rename or delete a folder you made: right-click it. Built-in folders such as Inbox and Sent Items can't be renamed or deleted.

Note: Classic Outlook: right-click a folder › New Folder, or use the Folder tab › New Folder (Ctrl+Shift+E). Move messages with Home tab › Move.

Practice: Drag each message into the folder where you would file it.


## Favorites and Archive
Favorites, at the top of the folder pane, keeps the folders you open most within one click, no matter how long your folder list gets. Right-click a folder and choose Add to Favorites; right-click it in Favorites to remove it. Archive (Home tab) moves a finished message to the built-in Archive folder, where it stays searchable.

Note: Folders don't change who can see a message, and they don't replace your practice's records rules. Keep email about patient care, claims and referrals in the practice mailbox, never in personal email or a USB drive.

## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: How do you make a folder inside Insurance & Billing in new Outlook? Answer: Right-click Insurance & Billing › Create new subfolder. Right-click the folder that will hold the new one and choose Create new subfolder.

Check yourself: Which message should stay in the Inbox? Answer: A physician's request to call a patient back today. The Inbox holds work that isn't done yet. The callback still needs you.
