## Cropping is editing, not trimming
 type: scenario
 role: You are building a staff directory at Lakeside Medical Associates.
 text: Eleven staff photos arrive from eleven different phones. Some are portrait, some landscape, some show the whole waiting room with a person somewhere in it. Dropped into the directory as-is, they make the page look like a scrapbook. The document is going to new patients.
 task: Crop and adjust the photos so they read as one consistent set.

Cropping serves composition, not just size. Cropping every headshot to the same aspect ratio and framing each face in roughly the same position makes eleven unrelated photos read as one deliberate set. Resizing alone cannot do that — a wide photo scaled down is still a wide photo of a waiting room.

Compression is the practical companion. A modern phone photo can be several megabytes; eleven of them produce a document too large to email. Picture Format > Compress Pictures reduces resolution to what the output actually needs. Use 220 ppi for something that will be printed, 150 ppi for on-screen reading, and 96 ppi for email. Always uncheck 'Apply only to this picture' when you want the whole document reduced.

Note: Cropping hides the cropped-away area; it does not delete it by default. Anyone who receives the file can drag the crop handles back out and reveal what you removed. If you cropped to hide something — another patient in the background, a name badge, a screen with data on it — you must also run Compress Pictures with 'Delete cropped areas of pictures' checked. Otherwise you have concealed it visually while still shipping the pixels.

Crop to Shape (Crop dropdown > Crop to Shape) trims the image to a circle or rounded rectangle in one step. Circular headshots are a quick, reliable way to make photos of mixed quality look coordinated.


## Introduction to Formatting Pictures
Microsoft Word provides powerful tools for formatting pictures. You can crop images, apply artistic effects, adjust brightness and color, add borders, and compress pictures to reduce file size.


## Cropping an Image
Cropping removes unwanted areas of an image. Select the image, go to the Picture Format tab, and click Crop.


## Crop to Shape
You can crop a picture into a specific shape. Click Crop → Crop to Shape and select a shape such as circle, star, or banner.


## Adding a Picture Border
You can add a colored border to any image. Use Picture Border to choose a color, weight (thickness), and line style.


## Using Corrections
The Corrections tool allows you to sharpen or soften an image and adjust brightness and contrast.


## Adjusting Color
The Color tool allows you to adjust saturation (vibrancy), tone (warm or cool), and recolor the entire image.


## Applying Artistic Effects
Artistic Effects apply stylized filters such as pastel, watercolor, or glow. These should be used carefully in professional documents.


## Using Picture Styles
Picture Styles are predefined formats that quickly apply frames, shadows, and visual effects without manually adjusting settings.


## Compressing Pictures
Large images increase document size. Use Compress Pictures to reduce resolution and delete cropped areas.

## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: What tool would you use to remove the outer edge of an image? Answer: Crop. Crop trims away the outer parts of an image so you keep only the region you want. Note that by default Word hides rather than discards the cropped area — use Compress Pictures with 'Delete cropped areas' to remove it for good.
