Comments

Module 4: References, Review & Collaboration

Add, reply to, resolve, and delete comments in Word to communicate feedback and collaborate on documents without altering the main content.

By the end of this lesson you can:
- Insert, reply to, resolve, and delete comments
- Explain the difference between resolving and deleting a comment
- Navigate between comments efficiently during a review
- Write comments that a colleague can act on without asking a follow-up question


## Comments are a conversation with a future reader

 type: scenario
 role: You are reviewing a colleague's intake form revision at Lakeside Medical Associates.
 text: You leave a comment that says 'This is confusing.' Your colleague opens it three days later reads the paragraph five times cannot work out which part you meant or what would fix it and either guesses or comes to find you. Either way the comment cost more time than it saved.
 task: Write comments specific enough to act on and manage the review so nothing is silently lost.


A comment attaches a note to a specific selection without altering the text. That specificity is its value — select the exact phrase you mean, not the whole paragraph, so the reader knows precisely what you are pointing at.

Say what is wrong and what would resolve it. 'This is confusing' gives the author nothing. 'Does "within 24 hours" mean business hours or calendar hours? Suggest stating it explicitly' names the ambiguity and proposes the fix, and it can be actioned in thirty seconds without a conversation.

Note: Resolving and deleting are different. A resolved thread is marked Resolved and stays in the document, preserving the record of what was raised and that it was addressed. A deleted thread is gone. During an active review, resolve rather than delete — the thread is the evidence of why the document says what it says. Delete everything only in the final cleanup before the document leaves the practice.

Ctrl+Alt+M inserts a comment on the current selection. Review > Previous and Next step through comments in order, which is the reliable way to make sure none is missed in a long document — scrolling and looking is not.


## What Are Comments?
Comments let you attach notes to specific text in a document without changing the content. They appear as comment cards beside the text, or in the Comments pane, and are ideal for feedback, questions, and review notes.


## Inserting a Comment
Select the text you want to comment on, then go to Review → New Comment (or press Ctrl+Alt+M). A comment card opens beside your selected text. Type your note, then select Post comment (or press Ctrl+Enter).


## Replying to a Comment
Click the Reply box inside an existing comment card, type your reply, and select the Post comment button (or press Ctrl+Enter) to add a threaded reply. This creates a conversation thread attached to the same text.


## Resolving a Comment
Select More thread actions (…) on the comment card and choose Resolve thread. The thread is marked Resolved and stays in the document. In the Comments pane's default Contextual view, resolved threads are hidden from the margin; switch to List view (Review > Show Comments > List) to see every comment, resolved ones included.


## Deleting a Comment
To permanently remove a comment, select More thread actions (…) on the comment card and choose Delete thread, or click in the commented text and select Delete on the Review tab. To remove every comment at once, select the arrow under Delete and choose Delete All Comments in Document.


## Navigating Between Comments
Use the Previous and Next buttons in the Comments group on the Review tab to jump between comments without scrolling through the document manually.


## Knowledge Check
Check yourself: What happens when you resolve a comment in Word? Answer: The thread is marked Resolved and stays in the document as a record. Resolving marks the thread as dealt with while keeping it in the document as a record of what was raised and settled. It drops out of the margin in the default view but still appears in the Comments pane's List view. Deleting removes it entirely, along with that history.
