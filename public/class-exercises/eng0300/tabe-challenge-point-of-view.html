Read the passage

This is a new passage, not one from the practice pages. It mixes facts and opinions, sometimes in the same paragraph. Read all of it, twice if you can, before you decide what the author wants you to think.

What Shirley Chisholm Started

Biography · 285 words

1In 1968, voters in Brooklyn elected Shirley Chisholm to the United States House of Representatives. She was the first Black woman ever elected to Congress. Before she ran for office, she had spent years as a nursery school teacher and as the director of a child care center. No training could have prepared a lawmaker better for the work ahead.

2Chisholm served seven terms in the House, from 1969 to 1983. Early on, House leaders placed her on the Agriculture Committee, an odd assignment for a member from a city district. She objected and was moved to a different committee. During her years in Congress she worked to expand the food stamp program, and she helped create WIC, the program that helps low-income mothers, infants, and young children get healthy food.

3Most people who know her name remember one thing. In 1972 she became the first Black candidate to seek a major party's nomination for President, and the first woman to seek the Democratic nomination. Her slogan was "Unbought and Unbossed." Remembering only the campaign, though, sells her short. A campaign lasts a season. The programs she fought for still feed families every day.

4It is true that Chisholm did not come close to winning. She received 152 delegate votes at the Democratic convention, a small share of the total. But a first campaign is not measured only by its vote count, and it would be a mistake to call hers a failure.

5Chisholm left Congress in 1983 and went on to teach college students. She died in 2005. Ten years later, she was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Her work in Congress deserves to be as well known as her run for President.

STEP 3 Write

Question 4: short response

In 3 to 4 full sentences, explain how the author's point of view toward Chisholm shapes this passage.

What a strong answer looks like: it makes three moves.

  1. It names the author's point of view toward Chisholm.
  2. It quotes one statement of fact and one statement of opinion from the passage.
  3. It explains one thing the author emphasizes or plays down because of that point of view.

0 sentences so far. Aim for 3 to 4.

Self-check. Tap each line when your answer does it:

Model answer: The author admires Chisholm, and that point of view decides which facts get the most room. The passage rests on checkable facts such as "Chisholm served seven terms in the House, from 1969 to 1983," but it adds judgments like "No training could have prepared a lawmaker better for the work ahead." Because of that point of view, her work in Congress is praised in paragraph 3 and again in the last sentence, while the fact that she "received 152 delegate votes" is stated once and excused in the very next sentence.
