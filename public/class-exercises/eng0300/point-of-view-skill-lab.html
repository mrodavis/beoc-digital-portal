From an Employee Handbook

Workplace · 70 words

You must submit your time sheet by 5:00 p.m. on the Friday that ends the pay period. If you are scheduled off that Friday, submit it on your last worked day of the week. Late time sheets are processed in the following cycle, which means your hours will appear on the next paycheck rather than the current one. Keep a copy of your submission confirmation until the corresponding paycheck arrives.

PART 2 Practice

Practice on a full passage

A longer passage with three questions. Check each answer as you go.

What the Library Became

Argument · 171 words

1The branch library on my corner circulated 41,000 items last year. It also hosted 312 public programs, provided 9,400 sessions on its public computers, and served as a designated cooling center on eleven days.

2City budget discussions still describe library funding as spending on books. That description is decades out of date, and it quietly makes the case for cuts. If a library is a book warehouse, then a city with declining print circulation has an obvious place to save money.

3But the computer sessions are not incidental. For a resident applying for a job, renewing a benefit, or filing a form that no longer exists on paper, the branch is often the only free place to sit down with a keyboard and get help using it.

4I do not think the library should be exempt from budget scrutiny. Every department should have to justify what it costs. I think the justification should be measured against what the library actually does, and the current accounting does not measure that at all.

PART 3 Mastery Check

Check yourself

Two short passages about the same event, four questions. If you miss one, the note under the answer tells you what to review.

Passage 1: A Resident's Letter

Argument · 103 words

The elevator in my building was out of service for nine days this month. I live on the sixth floor, and I managed the stairs. My neighbor across the hall uses a cane, and for those nine days she did not leave her apartment. We were given one notice, taped inside the lobby door, and it gave no date for the repair. I understand that parts take time. What I do not understand is why no one from the office knocked on a single door to ask who needed help getting groceries upstairs. A building this size should have a plan for this.

Original passage · BEOC Academic Bridge

Passage 2: Notice from the Management Office

Informational · 93 words

The building's elevator was taken out of service on March 4 after a routine inspection found a worn part in the motor. Because the elevator is more than thirty years old, the replacement part had to be made to order. The elevator returned to service on March 13 and passed a city safety inspection the same day. A notice was posted in the lobby on the first day of the outage. The repair was completed at no cost to residents. Management handled the repair as quickly and carefully as residents could reasonably expect.
