PART 2 15 minutes · guided example

Guided Example: Why the Waiting Room Filled Up

"On Tuesday morning, the clinic's check-in computer froze. Because staff could not pull up appointments, every patient had to be signed in by hand. As a result, check-in took four minutes per person instead of one. The waiting room filled quickly, and by ten o'clock patients were standing in the hallway. The slowdown also affected the pharmacy, since prescriptions could not be sent electronically. One frozen screen, in other words, produced a chain of delays that reached every corner of the building."

Step 1: circle the signal words in your notebook. Step 2: name the pattern. Step 3: one cause, three effects. Step 4: predict what the next paragraph would talk about.

Signals: Because, As a result, since, produced. Pattern: cause and effect. Cause: the check-in computer froze. Effects: slow check-in, a crowded waiting room, pharmacy delays. Prediction: the fix, which would shift the passage toward problem and solution.
PART 3 25 minutes✓ Complete

Practice Set A: Name That Pattern

Notebook first: pattern + two signal words that prove it. Then tap your answer.

Passage A. "Marisol decided to become a medical assistant in January. First, she enrolled in an evening program near her job. By spring, she had finished her classroom hours and moved on to a clinical placement. During the summer she studied for her certification exam, reviewing flashcards on the train each morning. Finally, in September, she passed the exam and posted her certificate above her desk. The whole journey took nine months from decision to diploma."
Chronology. Proof: First, By spring, During the summer, Finally.
Passage B. "Some students take notes on paper, while others type them on a laptop. Paper notes are slower to write, but that slowness forces the writer to choose only the most important ideas. Typed notes, on the other hand, capture almost everything, yet they are easy to forget because little thinking happens while copying. Both methods can work. The difference lies in what happens afterward: paper notes are usually reread, whereas typed notes often sit unopened in a folder."
Comparison. Proof: while, on the other hand, whereas, Both.
Passage C. "Riverside Clinic had a problem: nearly one in five patients missed their appointments each week. Missed visits wasted staff time and left other patients waiting weeks for an opening. The office manager proposed a solution. The clinic began sending text reminders two days before each visit and again that morning. Patients could confirm or rebook with one tap. Within two months, no-shows fell by half, and the schedule finally matched the people who actually walked through the door."
Problem and solution. Proof: had a problem, proposed a solution, Within two months.
Passage D. "Last month the city rerouted the B41 bus away from Halsey Street. Because the nearest stop moved six blocks, many older riders in the neighborhood stopped taking the bus at all. Local shops felt the change immediately, since foot traffic thinned out by early evening. The reroute also caused crowding on the B44, which now carries riders from both corridors. One decision at the transit office, in short, set off effects that reached shopkeepers, riders, and a second bus line."
Cause and effect. Proof: Because, since, caused, set off.
PART 4 20 minutes✓ Complete

Practice Set B: TABE-Style Questions

"At Mercy Home Care, the day team and the night team once shared information through sticky notes left on a bulletin board. Notes fell off, handwriting was misread, and twice in one month a client's medication change never reached the night nurse. The agency needed a safer handoff. The solution came from a supervisor who had worked in a hospital: a fifteen-minute overlap shift. Now the day aide stays until the night aide arrives, and the two walk through each client's chart together. Since the change, no medication updates have been missed. The overlap costs the agency a small amount of extra pay each week, but managers consider it the cheapest insurance they have ever bought."

1. Which pattern organizes the passage overall?

A problem (missed medication changes) is described, then a solution (the overlap shift) and its results.

2. The first two sentences mostly describe:

Falling notes, misread handwriting, and a missed medication change are the problem.

3. Which words signal that the problem has been fixed?

"Since the change" points at the result of the solution.

4. Why does the author include the sentence about extra pay?

"The cheapest insurance they have ever bought" weighs the cost against the benefit.

5. If the passage continued, which sentence would most likely come next?

Problem-and-solution passages usually extend the solution next. Structure lets you predict.

Mixed Review Sprint: The First Four Skills

Main idea, inference, vocabulary in context, and structure, all on this page's passages (Parts 3 and 4). It is a warm-up for the Mid-Course Progress Check.

1. Which sentence best states the main idea of Passage C?

The main idea covers the whole passage: problem, reminder system, result.

2. From Passage D you can infer that before the reroute, many older riders:

Shops lost foot traffic when riders stopped coming, so riders had been reaching the shops by bus.

3. In Passage D, "corridors" most nearly means:

Multi-meaning word: context ("carries riders from both corridors") points to routes.

4. In the Mercy passage, "handoff" most nearly means:

The whole passage is about how information moves between the day and night teams.

5. The clinic most likely sends the second text on the morning of the visit because:

Inference: the passage never says why - you reason it out from the purpose of reminders.

6. Which detail from Passage C shows the solution worked?

Evidence of the result, not the problem or the mechanism.

7. The author of the Mercy passage would most likely agree that:

"The cheapest insurance they have ever bought" carries the author's point of view.

8. Passage A is organized by chronology. That structure best helps a reader answer which question?

Time order tells you when and how long - that is what the structure is built to show.
