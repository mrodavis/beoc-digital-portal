  • Work out an unfamiliar word from the sentences around it.
  • Answer TABE-format reading questions under time pressure.
  • Turn a reading observation into a clear written paragraph.

Station 1 — Main Ideas & Details (30 minutes)

Passage A — The Route That Came Back

When the city cut the number 12 bus in 2021, the decision looked reasonable on paper. Ridership had fallen to fourteen passengers per hour, the lowest of any route in the system, and the buses ran nearly empty for most of the afternoon. The transit agency moved the vehicles to busier corridors and told riders to use the number 7, four blocks away.

Four blocks is not far on a map. It is a different distance in practice. The number 12 had served a stretch of low-rise housing where a third of households have no car, and where many residents are older adults or people working long shifts at the hospital on the north side. Within a year, the clinic on Halsey Street reported that missed appointments had climbed by nearly half. A grocery store near the old route closed. The complaints that reached the agency were not about convenience. They were about access.

In 2024 the agency restored the route, but not the way it had run before. Instead of one bus every thirty minutes all day, the number 12 now runs every twelve minutes during the morning and evening peaks and drops to hourly service midday. Ridership reached twenty-nine passengers per hour within six months — double what it had been when the route was cut.

The agency's own report drew a careful conclusion. The problem had never been that nobody wanted the bus. The problem was that a bus every thirty minutes is not transportation; it is a gamble. Riders had abandoned the route because the schedule did not fit their lives, and the low numbers that justified the cut were a symptom of the schedule, not a verdict on the neighborhood.

  1. Which sentence best states the main idea of Passage A?
    A. The number 12 bus was cut in 2021 and restored in 2024.
    B. Low ridership numbers measured a bad schedule, not a neighborhood that did not want service.
    C. Four blocks is farther than it looks on a map.
    D. Transit agencies should run buses every twelve minutes.
  2. The passage says ridership reached twenty-nine passengers per hour. Is that the main idea or a supporting detail? Answer in one sentence, and say what it supports.
  3. List two details the writer uses to show that “four blocks” mattered more than it appeared to on a map.
  4. Summarize Passage A in one sentence of 25 words or fewer. Use your own words.
  5. If you had to cut one paragraph from this passage, which would you cut, and what would the passage lose?

Station 2 — Inferences & Conclusions (30 minutes)

Passage B — What the Numbers Did Not Say

Marcus had managed the hardware store on Fulton for six years, and he knew the Saturday rush the way he knew his own hands. So when the regional office sent a memo cutting Saturday staff from five to three, he did not argue with the numbers in it. Sales per employee-hour were lower on Saturday than on Tuesday. That much was true.

What the memo did not contain was the shape of a Saturday. Saturday customers were not the contractors who came in Tuesday morning with a list and a truck. They were homeowners, and homeowners ask questions. A homeowner replacing a bathroom fitting needs someone to walk to aisle nine, explain the difference between two parts, and check whether the seal is included. That conversation takes eleven minutes. The contractor's purchase takes ninety seconds.

After the cut, Marcus watched the line build at the register while two of his three staff were somewhere in the aisles, mid-conversation. Some customers set their items on a shelf and walked out. The store's Saturday sales fell nine percent over the next two months. Sales per employee-hour, however, went up.

Marcus wrote back to the regional office. He did not dispute a single figure in their memo. He asked them one question instead: which number were they trying to improve?

  1. Marcus never says the regional office was wrong. What does he believe they measured badly, and which sentences let you conclude it?
  2. Label each statement STATED or IMPLIED:
    a. Saturday sales fell nine percent.
    b. The regional office had probably never worked a Saturday in this store.
    c. Contractors shop on Tuesday mornings.
    d. Marcus thinks total sales matter more than sales per employee-hour.
  3. Explain how sales per employee-hour could go up in the same two months that total sales went down. Two sentences maximum.
  4. The passage ends with a question rather than a statement. What does that choice do that a flat statement would not?
  5. Across both passages: what mistake do the transit agency and the regional office have in common? Answer in two or three sentences, using one detail from each passage.

Station 3 — Vocabulary in Context (25 minutes)

Part 1 — Context clues. For each word, give its meaning in the passage and name the clue type you used: definition, example, contrast, or general sense.

  • corridors — “moved the vehicles to busier corridors” (Passage A)
  • symptom — “a symptom of the schedule, not a verdict on the neighborhood” (Passage A)
  • verdict — same sentence as above (Passage A)
  • abandoned — “Riders had abandoned the route” (Passage A)
  • dispute — “He did not dispute a single figure” (Passage B)

Part 2 — Roots and prefixes. Give the meaning of each part, then find one more word in either passage that uses it.

  • trans– as in transit, transportation
  • re– as in restored, reported
  • dis– as in dispute

Station 4 — TABE-Style Timed Set (20 minutes)

15 minutes to answer, 5 minutes to review your answers. Work alone. Do not look back at Stations 1–3.

Passage C — The Shelf Nobody Checked

The Eastbrook Food Pantry serves about four hundred families a month. For years, volunteers stocked shelves by category — canned goods in one aisle, pasta in another, baby supplies at the back. The system was tidy, and the pantry director assumed it worked.

In 2025 a volunteer named Dolores began keeping a simple count of what went home and what stayed. The results surprised everyone. Canned vegetables, the most heavily stocked item in the pantry, moved slowly. Rice and dried beans disappeared within hours of restocking. Baby formula, kept at the back because it was expensive, ran out before most families ever reached that aisle.

The pantry made three changes. Rice and beans moved to the front. Baby formula moved to a staffed counter, where every family was told it was available. Canned vegetables were reduced by a third, and the savings went toward the items that actually left the building.

Six months later the pantry was serving the same four hundred families on a budget that had not grown, and the amount of food discarded at the end of each month had dropped by more than half. Dolores's count had cost nothing. It had simply replaced an assumption with a number.

  1. What is the main idea of Passage C?
    A. Food pantries should stock less canned food.
    B. Counting what people actually took revealed problems a tidy system had hidden.
    C. Dolores was the pantry's most valuable volunteer.
    D. The pantry needed a larger budget.
  2. Which detail best supports the main idea?
    A. The pantry serves about four hundred families a month.
    B. Baby formula was expensive.
    C. Food discarded dropped by more than half on the same budget.
    D. Volunteers stocked shelves by category.
  3. Why was baby formula running out before most families reached it?
    A. The pantry did not order enough.
    B. Its position at the back meant only some families got that far.
    C. Families did not know it existed.
    D. Volunteers took it first.
  4. In this passage, assumed most nearly means
    A. proved through testing
    B. took for granted without checking
    C. announced publicly
    D. hoped quietly
  5. The prefix in restocking tells you the shelves were being filled
    A. for the first time
    B. again
    C. incorrectly
    D. in advance
  6. Which conclusion is best supported by the passage?
    A. Organizing by category is always a mistake.
    B. An orderly system can still be measuring the wrong thing.
    C. Volunteers know more than directors.
    D. The pantry should stop stocking canned vegetables.
  7. The writer includes the last two sentences mainly to
    A. praise Dolores by name
    B. show that the fix required no money, only information
    C. explain how the count was recorded
    D. criticize the pantry director
  8. In one sentence of 20 words or fewer, summarize what the pantry learned.
