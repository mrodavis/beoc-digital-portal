PART 1 10 minutes ✓ Complete

Warm-Up: Pattern Flash

Read each sentence. Tap the pattern it uses. Look for the signal words.

Notebook first: number 1 to 8 and write the pattern for each sentence before you tap.

1. “First, wash your hands. Next, put on clean gloves. Finally, check the patient's ID band.”

Chronology. "First," "Next," and "Finally" put the steps in time order.

2. “Unlike a résumé, a cover letter is written in full paragraphs.”

Comparison. "Unlike" shows how two things are different.

3. “Because the train was delayed, Maria missed the start of her job interview.”

Cause and effect. "Because" names the cause (the delay). Missing the interview is the effect.

4. “Patients were waiting on hold for twenty minutes. To fix this, the clinic added online booking.”

Problem and solution. First a problem (long waits), then "To fix this" introduces the solution.

5. “In 2021, Ana started as a cashier. Two years later, she became a shift supervisor.”

Chronology. A date and "Two years later" move the reader through time.

6. “Both the bus and the subway cost the same fare, but the subway is usually faster at rush hour.”

Comparison. "Both" shows how they are alike. "But" shows how they are different.

7. “The storm knocked out power on three blocks. As a result, the corner store lost all of its frozen food.”

Cause and effect. "As a result" connects the cause (no power) to the effect (lost food).

8. “Our team kept missing deadlines. The solution was a shared calendar that sends reminders.”

Problem and solution. "The solution was" is a direct signal.

Guided Example: Two Texts, One Topic

Both texts below are about cooling centers. Same topic, different builds. As you read, fill in the chart in your notebook.

TEXT A: A Hot Day at the Marcy Avenue Cooling CenterAt 9:00 a.m. on July 21, the thermometer outside the Marcy Avenue library already read 88 degrees. By 10:00, the staff had set up forty folding chairs, two water coolers, and a table of paper fans. The first visitor, a retired bus driver named Mr. Ortiz, arrived at 10:15 carrying a bottle of frozen water. Around noon, a mother came in with twin toddlers whose cheeks were bright red. By 3:00 p.m., every chair was filled, and a volunteer began handing out cold towels. When the doors finally closed at 7:00 p.m., the staff counted 112 visitors, the most in the center's history.
TEXT B: Keeping Neighbors Safe When the City Heats UpEvery summer, extreme heat sends many city residents to the hospital. Older adults and people without air conditioning face the greatest danger, because their bodies cannot cool down overnight. One answer has been cooling centers: libraries, senior centers, and community rooms that open their air-conditioned space to anyone during a heat emergency. To make the centers work, the city posts their locations online, and neighbors are asked to check on older residents and bring them in. Health workers say that a few hours in a cool room can lower body temperature enough to prevent heat illness.
Notebook chart: make two columns, Text A and Text B. Fill in four rows: Pattern, Signal words, What the reader notices, and Author's purpose.

1. How is Text A organized?

The text moves hour by hour: "At 9:00 a.m.," "By 10:00," "Around noon," "By 3:00 p.m.," "When the doors finally closed."

2. How is Text B organized?

The first two sentences name a problem (heat is dangerous). Then "One answer has been cooling centers" names the solution.

3. What does Text A help a reader notice that Text B does not?

Choices A, C, and D all come from Text B. Only Text A follows one real day from morning to night.

4. Which statement best compares the two texts?

A good comparison names both structures and shows the difference. That is the move you will make in Part 6.

Practice Pair 1: Two Ways to Take a Class

Read both texts. Then answer the four questions in your notebook before you tap.

TEXT A: Night Classes or Online Classes?Adults who want to earn a certificate often choose between night classes and online classes. Both options let students keep a daytime job, and both lead to the same certificate. However, the two differ in important ways. Night classes meet at a set time in a classroom, so students can ask the instructor questions face to face. Online classes, on the other hand, can be done at any hour, which helps parents who cannot find evening childcare. Night students must travel to campus, while online students need a working computer and internet at home. Similarly, both options take discipline, but online students must set their own schedule with no one reminding them.
TEXT B: What Happened When the Classes Moved OnlineLast spring, a burst pipe flooded the first floor of the Eastside Training Center, and the building closed for three months of repairs. Because the classrooms could not be used, every class moved online. As a result, many students who had never taken an online class had to learn new tools quickly. Some fell behind at first, so the center set up a phone help line. The change also had an effect no one expected: when the building reopened, one out of three students asked to stay online, because studying at home saved them hours of travel each week.

1. Text A is mainly organized by:

Text A shows how night and online classes are alike ("Both," "Similarly") and different ("However," "on the other hand," "while").

2. Which group of signal words shows the pattern of Text B?

These words link causes to effects: the building closed, so classes moved online; as a result, students had to learn new tools.

3. According to Text B, what was one effect that no one expected?

The text says it directly: "The change also had an effect no one expected: ... one out of three students asked to stay online." Choice D never appears in the text.

4. A student is deciding which kind of class to sign up for. Which text is more useful, and why?

A comparison structure is built for choosing between options. Text B explains what happened after a flood; it does not help you choose.

Practice Pair 2: TABE-Style Questions

On the TABE, paired passages often come with questions that ask you to use both texts. Read both, then answer in your notebook.

TEXT A: A Fridge on the CornerIn the blocks around Linden Street, many families struggled to afford fresh food at the end of each month, while nearby restaurants threw away unsold meals every night. A group of neighbors saw both problems at once. Their solution was simple: a community refrigerator placed outside a laundromat, where anyone could leave food and anyone could take it. Volunteers clean the fridge every morning and remove anything past its date. Now restaurants drop off extra trays, and families stop by without filling out a form or explaining themselves.
TEXT B: One Year of the Linden Street FridgeThe Linden Street fridge was plugged in on a cold morning in January. For the first two weeks, it sat almost empty, holding only a few cartons of milk. In February, a bakery on the corner began leaving its day-old bread each evening. By spring, three restaurants had joined, and volunteers made a sign-up sheet for cleaning shifts. During the summer heat, the group added a second fridge beside the first. Finally, in December, the neighbors held a party to celebrate twelve months and more than 5,000 meals shared.

1. Which pattern organizes Text A?

Text A opens with two problems (families cannot afford fresh food; restaurants throw food away). Then "Their solution was simple" introduces the fix.

2. Which words from Text B best signal its structure?

Text B follows the fridge month by month, so its signal words are time words. Choices B, C, and D come from Text A.

3. Which detail appears in Text B but NOT in Text A?

Both texts mention restaurants and volunteers. Only Text B, which tracks the whole year, mentions the second fridge. Check every choice against both texts.

4. How are the purposes of the two texts different?

Problem and solution explains why and how. Chronology shows change over time.

Progress Check Prep: Mixed Review

The Mid-Course Progress Check covers the first four reading skills: main ideas, inferences, vocabulary, and text structure. This set has two questions for each skill. Each question shows which skill it tests.

Notebook: write your answer letters 1 to 8. After you check, circle every question you missed. Those are the skills to review next.
THE NIGHT SHIFT AT RIVERSIDEMost shoppers never think about how a package reaches their door by morning. At the Riverside Distribution Center, the work begins after sunset. At 10:00 p.m., trucks back up to the loading docks, and forty workers start moving boxes onto long belts. Scanners read each label, and the belts carry every box to one of twelve lanes, one lane for each delivery route. The job is demanding. Workers lift boxes that weigh up to fifty pounds, and in July the building is a furnace. Still, many workers stay for years. Denise Hall, a shift leader, started as a temporary worker and has been promoted twice. “The pay differential for nights is what let me finish my certificate during the day,” she says. By 5:00 a.m., the last truck pulls away, loaded and sealed. When the city wakes up, the packages are already on their way.

MAIN IDEA
1. What is the passage mainly about?

Most sentences describe the night's work from 10:00 p.m. to 5:00 a.m. Choices B and D are details, not the main idea.

SUPPORTING DETAILS
2. Which detail supports the idea that "the job is demanding"?

Heavy lifting shows the work is hard. The other details are true but do not show that the job is demanding.

VOCABULARY IN CONTEXT
3. In the passage, "pay differential" most likely means:

Clue: "for nights." Denise says the night pay is what let her finish school, so it must be something extra she earns for working nights.

FIGURATIVE LANGUAGE
4. The author writes, "in July the building is a furnace." This means:

This is a metaphor. The building is not really a furnace; it is compared to one to show how hot it gets.

INFERENCES
5. What can you infer about the workers who "stay for years"?

The author says the job is demanding, then writes "Still, many workers stay." Denise's story shows benefits: promotions and pay that helped her finish school. Put those clues together.

CONCLUSIONS
6. Which conclusion is best supported by the passage?

The passage shows the night work ending at 5:00 a.m. so that packages are "already on their way" when the city wakes up. Choice D goes against the first sentence.

TEXT STRUCTURE
7. How is most of the passage organized?

The passage follows the night from sunset to morning.

SIGNAL WORDS
8. Which words best signal that structure?

Times and time phrases are the signal words of chronology.

Write It: Compare the Builds

Choose Pair 1 (night vs. online classes) or Pair 2 (the Linden Street fridge). Write 5 to 7 sentences that compare how the two texts are organized.

Sentence frames (use them or write your own):
  1. Text A and Text B are both about ______, but they are organized in different ways.
  2. Text A uses ______. For example, the author uses the signal words "______" and "______."
  3. In contrast, Text B uses ______. The words "______" show this.
  4. Text A helps the reader notice ______, while Text B helps the reader notice ______.
  5. If I wanted to ______, I would read Text ___, because ______.

Comparison words: bothsimilarlyhoweverin contrastwhileon the other handunlike

Text A and Text B are both about cooling centers, but they are organized in different ways. Text A uses chronology. The author moves through one day with signal words like "At 9:00 a.m.," "Around noon," and "By 3:00 p.m." In contrast, Text B uses problem and solution. It starts with the danger of extreme heat, and then the words "One answer" introduce cooling centers as the solution. Text A helps the reader see how the crowd grew during a real day, while Text B helps the reader understand why cooling centers are needed. If I wanted to know how to stay safe in a heat wave, I would read Text B, because it explains the problem and the fix.

Self-check. Tap each line when your paragraph does it:

1My first sentence names the topic and says the texts are organized differently. (Focus)
2I named the pattern of each text. (Focus)
3I quoted at least two signal words, inside quotation marks. (Evidence)
4I used at least one comparison word: both, while, however, in contrast. (Organization)
5My last sentence says which text is more useful for a reader, and why. (Organization)
6I checked capital letters, periods, and spelling. (Conventions)
PART 7 10 minutes ✓ Complete

Exit Ticket

Three quick questions. Answer in your notebook, then tap.

1. Two passages are about the same topic but use different structures. What does that tell you?

Structure is a choice. The build shows you what the author wants you to notice.

2. "However," "on the other hand," and "while" usually signal:

These words point out a difference, so they signal comparison.

3. A TABE question asks, "Which detail appears in Passage 2 but not in Passage 1?" What is the best first step?

A detail can be true and still be in the wrong passage. Check each choice in both texts before you pick.
