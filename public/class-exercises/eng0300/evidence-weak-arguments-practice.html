PART 1 about 10 minutes · talk it out

Warm-Up: Claim or Fact?

A fact can be checked and proven. A claim is something a reasonable person could disagree with. Every argument starts with a claim.

Quick test: Could someone say "No, I disagree"? If yes, it is a claim.
PART 2 about 15 minutes · we do this one together · hand in

Claim, Reasons, Evidence

An argument has three parts. Learn these three words today. You will use them in your writing, too.

Claim
What the author wants you to believe. Someone could disagree.
Reason
Why the author believes it. A reason answers "Why?"
Evidence
The proof: facts, numbers, expert sources, or examples that back up the reason.

Read the passage. The sentences are numbered.

Every Medical Office Worker Should Know CPR

1 Every worker in a medical office, including front-desk and billing staff, should be trained in CPR. 2 A patient can collapse anywhere in the building, not just in an exam room. 3 In many offices, the first person to see a patient in trouble is the person at the front desk. 4 According to the American Heart Association, CPR given right away can double or triple a person's chance of survival. 5 A basic CPR class takes only a few hours, and many classes are finished in a single day. 6 For these reasons, training the whole staff is a small cost for a skill that can save a life.

Notebook: Number 1 to 4. Write your answer, then tap to check.

1. Which sentence states the author's claim?

Sentence 1 is the claim. It says what the author wants: CPR training for every worker. Someone could disagree, so it is a claim, not a fact.

2. Which sentence gives evidence from an expert source?

Sentence 4 names an expert source, the American Heart Association. Evidence from an expert source is usually strong evidence.

3. Sentence 3 is mainly a...

Sentence 3 answers "Why front-desk staff?" Because they are often the first person to see a patient in trouble. That is a reason.

4. Which fact, if true, would make this argument WEAKER?

If a trained nurse can always reach any patient in 30 seconds, the reason in Sentences 2 and 3 matters less. Strong readers ask: "What would make this argument weaker?"
PART 3 about 20 minutes · hand in

The Five Weak Spots

Some arguments sound good but do not hold up. Here are five weak spots to look for. Copy the five names into your notebook.

1. No ProofThe writer makes a claim but gives no evidence, or says "everyone knows.""This tea cures colds. Everyone knows it."Ask: Where is the proof?
2. Too BigThe writer uses one person or one story to say something about everyone."My cousin waited two hours at that clinic, so the clinic is terrible."Ask: Is one example enough?
3. Off TopicThe evidence is true, but it does not connect to the claim."Dr. Lee should run the clinic because she loves gardening."Ask: Does this detail support THIS claim?
4. Only Two ChoicesThe writer says there are only two options when there are more."Either we close on weekends, or the clinic goes out of business."Ask: Are there other choices?
5. Wrong CauseOne thing happened after another, so the writer says the first thing caused the second."I wore my lucky scrubs and passed the test, so the scrubs work."Ask: Could something else be the cause?

Name the Weak Spot

Now look at the four weak statements. Which weak spot does each one have? Write the name in your notebook.

5. "My neighbor said she would like evening hours."

Too Big. One neighbor is not enough to show what all patients need.

6. "Everyone knows evening hours are better."

No Proof. "Everyone knows" is not evidence. It gives no facts, numbers, or sources.

7. "Our clinic has the nicest waiting room in Brooklyn."

Off Topic. A nice waiting room has nothing to do with whether patients need evening hours.

8. "If we don't add evening hours, the clinic will have to close."

Only Two Choices. The clinic could also add Saturday hours, telehealth visits, or other options. The writer only shows two.
PART 4 about 20 minutes · on your own, then pair check · hand in

Practice Set: TABE-Style

Read each passage. Answer in your notebook first. Then check your answers with a partner. Then tap to check.

Passage A: A Letter to the Editor

Hospitals should not allow visitors after 8 PM. Last month, my father's roommate had visitors until 10 PM, and my father could not sleep. Patients need rest to heal. Either hospitals stop late visits, or patients will keep getting sicker. I hope hospital leaders will listen.

9. What is the writer's claim?

The claim is the first sentence. The other sentences try to support it.

10. The story about the father's roommate is weak evidence because...

This is the Too Big weak spot. One night in one room does not prove the problem is everywhere.

11. "Either hospitals stop late visits, or patients will keep getting sicker." Which weak spot is this?

Only Two Choices. Hospitals have other options, like quiet hours, ear plugs, or limits on how many visitors can come at night.

12. Which evidence would make this letter STRONGER?

A study of many patients is much stronger than one story. Parking spaces are off topic, and feelings are not evidence.
Passage B: Clean Hands, Fewer Infections

Germs can travel from one patient to another on the hands of health care workers. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) says that clean hands are one of the most important ways to prevent infections in health care settings. At one hospital, staff put hand sanitizer next to every patient room door and posted reminder signs. Over the next year, the number of infections at the hospital went down. The hospital director said the sanitizer stations were the reason. However, that same year, the hospital also hired more cleaning staff and started a new training program for nurses.

13. Which detail is the strongest evidence that clean hands help prevent infections?

The CDC is an expert national source, and it speaks about health care settings in general, not just one hospital.

14. The director says the sanitizer stations caused the drop in infections. Why is this NOT fully supported?

This is the Wrong Cause weak spot. More cleaning staff and new nurse training happened too. We cannot be sure the sanitizer alone made the difference.

15. Why did the author most likely include the last sentence, starting with "However"?

"However" signals a turn. The author wants the reader to think carefully before believing the director's claim.

PART 5 about 40 minutes · WRITE IT!

WRITE IT! CEE Paragraph, Draft 1

Your question: Look back at "Every Medical Office Worker Should Know CPR" in Part 2. Is the author's argument well supported?

Write one paragraph. Make a claim. Give two pieces of evidence from the passage. After each piece of evidence, explain how it supports your claim.

Claim
One sentence that answers the question. Someone could disagree with it.
Evidence 1
A short quote or detail from the passage. Name where it comes from.
Explanation 1
How does this evidence support your claim? This is the most important sentence. Do not skip it.
Evidence 2
A second quote or detail.
Explanation 2
How does this evidence support your claim?
Closing
One sentence that brings it back to your claim.
Sentence frames
  • The author's argument is (well supported / not fully supported) because ___. (claim)
  • The author writes, "___." (evidence)
  • This supports my claim because ___. (explanation)
  • Also, in sentence ___, the author states that ___. (evidence)
  • This shows that ___. (explanation)
  • For these reasons, ___. (closing)
claimevidenceexplanation
The argument in "Clean Hands, Fewer Infections" is only partly supported. The author writes that the CDC says clean hands are "one of the most important ways to prevent infections in health care settings." This supports the idea that handwashing matters, because the CDC is an expert source that studies many hospitals, not just one. However, the author also says that the hospital "hired more cleaning staff and started a new training program for nurses" in the same year. This shows that the director cannot be sure the sanitizer stations alone lowered infections, because other changes could be the cause. For these reasons, the main idea is strong, but the director's conclusion needs more proof.

Review Sprint

Five quick TABE-style questions. Use the timer: 2 minutes per question. Write the letter of your answer in your notebook, then tap to check.

16. Which detail best supports the claim "Walking every day is good for your health"?

B is research about health. A and D are off topic. C is only one person (Too Big).

17. "Everyone who works in a hospital is unhappy with their job." This statement is...

Words like "everyone," "all," "never," and "always" are a warning sign. They often mean the claim is Too Big.

18. Which sentence is a fact, not a claim?

A can be checked and proven. B, C, and D are things people could disagree with.

19. "Since the new manager started in January, the clinic phones have rung more. The new manager must be bringing in more patients." What is the problem with this reasoning?

Wrong Cause. The phones ringing more after January does not prove the manager is the reason. Winter is flu season.

20. Which evidence is most relevant to the claim "Our clinic needs a receptionist who speaks Spanish"?

C connects directly to the claim. It shows a real need. The others are off topic or opinions.
